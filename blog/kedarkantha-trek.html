Kedarkantha Trek | India’s Most Loved Winter Summit

Most Himalayan treks are about reaching a high-altitude lake or a valley, but Kedarkantha gives you the pure, adrenaline-pumping thrill of a proper summit climb. Located at 12,500 feet inside Uttarakhand’s Govind Wildlife Sanctuary, it is easily India’s most famous winter trek. It is massively popular because it gives a regular backpacker the raw thrill of a hardcore alpine expedition—without requiring a single technical climbing skill or rope.

The trail itself feels like a literal winter fairy tale. You will hike under thick pine and oak canopies heavily loaded with fresh snow. Sliding around the completely frozen Juda Ka Talab and pitching your tent right next to it makes the journey so incredibly immersive that you hardly even notice the physical fatigue. The real magic, however, is the summit push. Making that final steep climb in the freezing pre-dawn dark with just a headlamp will definitely test your lungs. But the moment you stand at the top, you are rewarded with a mind-blowing 360-degree panorama of Himalayan giants like Swargarohini, Black Peak, and Bandarpoonch.

Watching the sunrise light up those peaks from that altitude is a vibe you will remember for the rest of your life. You do not need any prior mountaineering experience or technical skills. A decent level of fitness is all you need to experience the rush of standing above the clouds.

Key Highlights

  • Most beginner trails end at a pass or a valley, but Kedarkantha gives you a genuine summit climb. The freezing early-morning push in the dark rewards you with a massive 360-degree sunrise view of Himalayan giants like Swargarohini and Black Peak.
  • From mid-December to March, the entire trail transforms into a flawless winter wonderland. Trekking through heavy, knee-deep snow inside dense pine forests is the biggest reason people flock here every winter.
  • You get to pitch your tent right next to the completely frozen Juda Ka Talab lake. The upper base camp is equally stunning, set in a clearing of snow-draped trees with an incredibly clear view of the Milky Way.
  • High Reward, Low Risk despite the intimidating 12,500 ft. elevation gain, this route is well-traveled and safe. While climbing the steep peak requires a good deal of stamina, it doesn't require any technical mountaineering skills, making it perfect for your first snow trek.

Mandatory Permits & Documents

  • Government Photo ID Proof (Original + Photocopy)
  • Govind Wildlife Sanctuary entry permits are available on-the-spot at the Sankri Village Forest Checkpost (no advance online booking required). A nominal entry fee (approximately ₹150 per person) and campsite charges (approximately ₹200 per night/tent) are payable in cash if camping.
  • Before departure, it's mandatory to complete a free biometric/tourist registration online on the Tourist Care Uttarakhand portal. Keep a digital slip or screenshot of this information on your phone.
  • UPI or card swiping is not reliable on the Sankari and beyond route (due to network issues), so keep cash with you for forest fees and local expenses.

Important Note for Travelers:

It is a common misconception that the Valley of Flowers and Hemkund Sahib are the exact same trek. While they do share the same base camp (Ghangaria), they are two completely different destinations. Once you reach Ghangaria, the trail splits into two opposite directions. One path leads you on a gentle walk into the colorful meadows of the Valley of Flowers, while the other takes you on a steep, challenging climb to the glacial lake and Gurudwara of Hemkund Sahib. You can easily cover both, but they require separate day trips from the base camp!

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before heading to the Kuari Pass Trek

It is widely considered India's most loved winter trek. The most special thing about it is the perfect, knee-deep snow trail through dense pine forests and the thrilling experience of a proper summit climb. Once you reach the top, you get a stunning 360-degree view of mighty Himalayan giants like Swargarohini, Black Peak, and Bandarpoonch.
Taking a guide is not legally required, and you can easily do it solo during the spring or autumn months. However, since this is primarily a winter trek (December to March), heavy snowfall often completely hides the trail, making navigation very tricky. During peak winter, hiring a local guide from Sankri base camp (around ₹1,000 per day) is highly recommended for your safety.
The total DIY budget from Dehradun to Dehradun comes to around ₹5,500 to ₹7,500. This easily covers your shared transport (bus or sumo) from Dehradun to Sankri, the Govind Pashu Vihar National Park forest permits, basic stays at the base camp, and your daily food on the trail. If you need to rent heavy winter gear like crampons or minus-degree sleeping bags from Sankri, it will add a little extra to this cost.
