Brahmatal Trek: A Winter Wonderland

Most winter treks keep you tucked inside deep valleys or heavy forests, but Brahmatal is a completely different game—it is all about walking on an exposed, snow-covered mountain ridge. If you want the absolute best, right-in-your-face views of massive Himalayan giants like Mt. Trishul and Mt. Nanda Ghunti, this is the trail I always recommend.

The journey takes you out of the Lohajung base camp, through ancient oak and rhododendron forests, and past two stunning high-altitude frozen lakes (Bekaltal and Brahmatal). When you finally push for the 12,200-foot summit, you are literally walking on a snowy ridgeline with a 360-degree panorama of snow-capped peaks surrounding you.

Even though you are hitting serious altitudes and navigating snow ridges, the gradient is surprisingly forgiving. It is an incredibly rewarding winter trek that demands basic fitness but absolutely zero technical mountaineering skills, making it a brilliant, less-crowded alternative to the Kedarkantha route.

According to local Himalayan mythology, Lord Brahma (the Hindu god of creation) meditated beside this exact high-altitude alpine lake thousands of years ago. Because this sacred, often frozen lake is the absolute heart of the journey and acts as your final major campsite before the summit push, the entire mountain ridge and the trail were named after it.

Key Highlights

  • Gives you the exact same hardcore winter snow and summit thrill as Kedarkantha, but with far fewer crowds for a much more peaceful experience.
  • You get to explore and camp near two totally different frozen lakes on the same trail—Bekaltal hidden under dense oak forests, and Brahmatal sitting wide open in a massive alpine meadow.
  • The final summit push is a thrilling, completely exposed walk along a snow-covered mountain ridge, making you feel like you are walking on top of the world.
  • Massive 7,000-meter Himalayan giants like Mt. Trishul and Mt. Nanda Ghunti appear so incredibly close from the top that you feel like you can literally reach out and touch them.

Mandatory Permits & Documents

  • Forest entry and camping permits are quickly cleared at the Lohajung forest checkpost. Just carry a physical copy of your original ID proof.
  • Always carry your original Aadhar, Voter ID, or Passport, along with 2-3 physical photocopies in your backpack.

Important Note for Travelers:

Micro-spikes, snow gaiters, and UV-sunglasses are absolute must-haves.
Pro-Tip: Cold air kills thirst. Force yourself to drink 3-4 liters of warm water daily to avoid altitude sickness.

Everything you need to know before heading to the Brahmatal Trek

The most special thing about this trek is its unique winter landscape featuring two fully frozen alpine lakes—Bekaltal and Brahmatal. It is also famous for its incredible, long ridge walk that gives you massive, uninterrupted, close-up views of Mt. Trishul and Mt. Nanda Ghunti that stay with you almost throughout the trail.
Taking a guide is not legally compulsory. During peak season, the trail is well-trodden and totally doable solo if you have basic trekking experience. However, if you are going right after a fresh winter snowfall, the exposed ridge gets completely covered in white, making navigation very tricky and dangerous. In that case, hiring a local guide from the Lohajung base camp for ₹1,000 to ₹1,200 per day is highly recommended.
The total DIY budget from Kathgodam (or Rishikesh) to Kathgodam comes to around ₹6,500 to ₹8,000. This easily covers your shared mountain taxis to the Lohajung base camp, forest camping permits, basic homestays at the base, and your daily trail food. If you need to rent heavy winter gear like sleeping bags or micro-spikes, you can easily get them from Lohajung, which will just add a little extra to this cost.
